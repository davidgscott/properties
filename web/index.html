    ❓ Help & Guide

    Screen land around Guerneville for a self-storage facility. Everything below is a screening aid — confirm anything promising with Permit Sonoma and FEMA before acting.

    Getting started (3 steps)

    1. Set the radius from Guerneville (the dashed circle on the map). You can also drag the blue pin to search around a different spot.
    2. Set your criteria — min acres, max slope, and the filters. Ticking Commercial / industrial vacant land only and Unincorporated county only gives the fastest, most focused run.
    3. Click Screen parcels and wait ~1–2 minutes (it checks flood, zoning & slope live). Results fill the map and table.

    How to read your results

    The list shows only the parcels worth a look — PASS and REVIEW. Parcels ruled out (in a flood zone, wrong zoning, too steep or too small) are screened out and only counted ("N screened out") — click show next to that count to reveal them, with the reason in the Notes column. If nothing qualifies you'll see "No qualifying parcels found."

    • PASS clears every filter — not in a flood zone, zoning permits storage (high confidence), flat, big enough. Your best leads.
    • REVIEW promising but needs a human look — zoning read to confirm, multiple zones, or missing slope data.

    Key columns: FEMA (X = outside the flood risk area = good), %SFHA (percent in a flood zone), Slope% (lower = flatter), Permit (by-right / use-permit / no). Each row links out to the county record, Google map, FEMA flood viewer, and the zoning code.

    Where the data comes from (free, official)

    ParcelsSonoma County GIS (assessor)
    ZoningPermit Sonoma zoning layer
    FloodFEMA National Flood Hazard Layer
    SlopeUSGS 3DEP elevation

    Data is pulled live each time you screen. No accounts or API keys; it's free to run.

    How reliable is it?

    FEMA flood data is the authoritative regulatory layer. Parcel and zoning data come straight from the county. Confidence refers to how sure the tool is that a zone permits storage: high for C3/MP/M2 (the code says so explicitly), medium for M1/M3 (a broader clause — verify). It does not check wetlands, access, utilities, title, or whether a parcel is actually for sale.
